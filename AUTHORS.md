# Authors

Opificia AI is written by The Opificia Authors. This file explains what that means.

## Who "The Opificia Authors" are

"The Opificia Authors" is everyone who has contributed to Opificia. The authoritative list is the git history of this repository: every person named in a commit, either as author or in a `Co-authored-by` trailer, is one of The Opificia Authors.

We use this collective name in copyright lines (`SPDX-FileCopyrightText: The Opificia Authors`) so that we do not have to update file headers every time someone new contributes.

## You keep your copyright

Opificia uses the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO), not a contributor licence agreement. When you sign off a commit, you certify that you have the right to submit it under the licence of the folder you change. You do not transfer your copyright to anyone. You remain the copyright holder of your contribution, and you license it to everyone under the same terms as the rest of that folder. [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) explains the DCO in full.

## How you are credited

- **In the git history.** This happens automatically when your commit is merged. Use the same name and email in every commit, or ask us to add an entry to [`.mailmap`](.mailmap) so your contributions are grouped together.
- **In release notes.** Each release lists the people who contributed to it.
- **In `MAINTAINERS.md`**, if you become a maintainer. See [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md).

Contributions that do not result in a commit, such as design reviews, translations reviewed elsewhere, or triage work, are just as welcome. We aim to credit them in release notes too. If we have missed you, open a pull request or a Discussion.

## Organisations

If your employer owns the copyright in your contributions, your sign-off certifies that you are allowed to contribute on their behalf. Organisations are not listed separately here. Maintainers disclose their affiliation in `MAINTAINERS.md`.
